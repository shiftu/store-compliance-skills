# Trader status declaration

Under Google Play’s global developer policies, all active developer accounts must declare whether they are a trader or non-trader under European Economic Area (EEA) and United Kingdom (UK) laws in Play Console. This declaration must be completed by February 1, 2027, regardless of where apps are targeted or distributed. The declaration must reflect whether you engage in commercial activities that classify you as a trader. You are solely legally responsible for ensuring that your declaration remains accurate, truthful, and current at all times.

**Note**: If you don’t distribute apps in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, you can declare as a non-trader.

## What is a trader?

Under applicable laws, a trader is **any natural or legal person acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft or profession.** If you are acting outside of these purposes (such as a personal hobby or other non-commercial activity), you are unlikely to be a trader.

## How to determine if you're a trader

To help you determine if your activities classify you as a trader, you may consider the following non-exhaustive list of criteria from the [European Commission guidance](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(05)) (Section 2.2, "The concept of trader"):

- **Profit-seeking motive**: you receive or intend to receive remuneration for your apps (for example, paid apps, in-app purchases, ad-sponsored apps) on a regular or commercial basis.
- **Transaction volume**: you have a high or consistent number, amount, or frequency of transactions.
- **Business setup**: you manage your app development and publishing as a business.
- **Legal status**: you have a legal status (such as a D-U-N-S number) that enables you to engage in commercial activities

In countries and regions where displaying trader status is required (for example, the EEA and the UK), your declaration will be visible to consumers. **If you declare yourself as a "non-trader," we will inform consumers in these regions that consumer rights stemming from consumer protection laws do not apply to contracts between you and them**. Note that under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) and the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act (DMCCA), it is strictly prohibited to falsely claim or create the impression that you are not acting as a trader, and doing so may result in global removal.

**Note**: This page is for informational guidance only and does not constitute legal advice. Google cannot evaluate your business activities or provide legal determinations of your trader status. If you are unsure whether you qualify as a trader, we recommend consulting with a legal professional.

## How to declare your trader or non-trader status

### Declare trader status for your account

First, set your account-level trader status settings:

1. Sign in to Google Play Console.
2. Go to **Manage account** > **Account details** > **About you**.
3. Follow the on-screen prompts to declare your trader status for your overall account.

### Confirm trader status for each app

If you are a trader, confirm the trader status for each app in your portfolio:

1. Sign in to Google Play Console and select your app.
2. In the left navigation, go to **Monitor and improve** > **Policy and programs** > **App content**.
3. Confirm the trader status for that app

## Additional steps you need to take if you’re a trader

If you declare as a trader, you will be asked to self-certify that you commit to only offer products or services that comply with applicable laws. You will also be asked to provide the link to the terms of service for each of your apps.

If you use a personal developer account, you must provide a contact phone number to be displayed on Google Play. Be aware that your address will also be publicly displayed.

## Status confirmation

To keep your apps available on Google Play, make sure your trader status declaration matches your account’s and apps’ activity. If you’ve declared as a non-trader but we detect activity that indicates you may qualify as a trader, we may ask you to confirm or update your status. If you don’t respond within the specified timeframe, your apps may be removed from Google Play.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Why do I need to submit a trader status declaration?

To support Google Play’s transition to an online marketplace model in the EEA and the UK effective March 1, 2027, Google Play policies require all developers worldwide to declare their trader status. This ensures that the correct consumer protection information is provided to users in those regions.

## Can I change my trader status after declaring?

Yes. If your business model or app distribution details change (for example, you begin or cease engaging in commercial activities), you can update your declaration at any time in Play Console.

## What happens if I don’t complete the declaration?

Failure to declare your trader status by the February 1, 2027 deadline may trigger enforcement including app update blocks and removal from the Play Store globally.

Misrepresenting your trader status (for example, declaring non-trader status to bypass verification or self-certification) violates the representations and warranties in Section 11.4 of the DDA and may result in account suspension or termination globally under Section 10.3 (a) of the DDA.

## What is the deadline to declare whether I am a trader?

All new developer accounts are required to submit the declaration immediately upon account creation. Existing developer accounts must submit the declaration by February 1, 2027.

## I already went through the developer verification D-U-N-S process, why am I doing it again?

The trader status declaration is separate to the existing developer identity verification process. The identity verification is a blanket requirement used to collect and verify information about every developer regardless of their trader status. You are not re-doing the D-U-N-S verification; instead, if you are a trader, the D-U-N-S number you already provided will be displayed to users on your app's detail page.

## What happens if I declare as a trader? What else gets shown to users in the Play store?

When you declare as a trader, you will be asked to self-certify that you commit to offering products or services that comply with local laws.

Starting March 1, 2027, users everywhere will see:

- **Contact details**: Your contact details will be shown. Notably, if you have an individual account and are a trader, a phone number and address will now be displayed.
- **D-U-N-S Number**: Your D-U-N-S number will be displayed in the "App support" section.
- **Terms of Service**: A link to your app’s Terms of Service, if you provided one.

Starting March 1, 2027, users in the EEA and UK will also see:

- **Developer name**: Your developer/trader name will replace Google in the "Offered by" field of the "About this app" section.
- **Self-certification**: Your commitment to offer products or services compliant with local laws will be displayed.
